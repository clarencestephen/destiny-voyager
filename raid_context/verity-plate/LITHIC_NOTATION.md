# Lithic Notation

**A visual philosophy.**

Lithic Notation treats an idea as something *quarried* rather than written — incised into a dark, mineral ground as though a forgotten exact science had left behind a single engraved plate. The surface is basalt: a near-black field with the depth of cooled stone, faintly grained, swallowing light at its edges so that everything luminous seems to have been *cut away* to reveal it. Form is the only vocabulary. Where another aesthetic would explain, this one inscribes — a circle, a solid, a hairline of measure — and trusts the patient viewer to read the geometry the way an astronomer reads a star chart. Text is rare and clinical, set as the curator's whisper: a catalog number, a Latinate label, an axiom no longer than a breath. Nothing is decorated; everything is *recorded*.

Its color is an act of restraint bordering on vow. A single warm vein — amber, the temperature of a struck match — moves through an otherwise bone-and-ash palette, and because it appears exactly once where it matters, it carries the entire emotional weight of the plate. Bone-white linework holds the structure with the steadiness of a master's graver; ash-grey carries the secondary notation; the void carries the silence. This is master-level chromatic discipline — the product of deep expertise that knows a second accent would halve the power of the first. The amber is not illustration. It is the thesis.

Scale and rhythm obey the logic of the specimen sheet. Primary forms are stated large and alone, given the reverence of a held note; their derivatives accumulate beneath in a measured grid, repeated with devotional patience until the repetition itself becomes the argument. Tick-marks file along the margins like the gradations of an instrument. Isometric solids are drawn with the painstaking attention of an engraver who has cut the same edge ten thousand times — each vertex deliberate, each line weighted, nothing approximate. The composition should read, on long inspection, as proof that something invisible can be *measured*.

Composition is symmetry held under tension — a central axis as exact as a plumb line, balanced by the quiet asymmetry of annotation drifting to one side, by negative space left vast and unhurried. Margins are sacred and never breached. Every element is locked to an underlying grid the eye never sees but the body feels, the way a cathedral feels true before you understand why. Hierarchy descends without noise: the monumental form, then its index, then the marginalia, then the lone caption set like an inscription at the base of a stele. The viewer's gaze is led, not herded.

Above all, Lithic Notation must look *laboured over* — meticulously crafted, the result of countless hours by someone at the absolute top of their field. It should carry the fingerprint of obsessive refinement: kerning corrected by hand, line weights tuned until they sing, geometry so clean it feels inevitable. The finished plate should feel less designed than *unearthed* — an artifact of such painstaking execution that the question of whether its discipline ever existed becomes, for a moment, beautifully irrelevant. This is the work of a master, and it must never look like anything less.
